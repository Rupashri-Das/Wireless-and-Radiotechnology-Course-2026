## 📖 Overview

This lab simulates and analyzes **thermal noise** (also called Johnson-Nyquist noise) using MATLAB. Thermal noise is generated by the random motion of electrons in a resistor due to temperature. It is one of the most fundamental noise sources in any electronic or wireless communication system.

The experiment covers:
- Generating thermal noise mathematically
- Visualizing it in the **time domain**
- Analyzing its **Power Spectral Density (PSD)**
- Confirming its **Gaussian amplitude distribution**

---

## 📐 Theory

Thermal noise voltage is modeled as:

$$v_{noise}(t) = \sqrt{4kTRB} \cdot n(t)$$

Where:
| Symbol | Meaning | Value Used |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| k | Boltzmann's constant | 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K |
| T | Temperature | 300 K (room temp) |
| R | Resistance | 100 Ω |
| B | Bandwidth | 1 MHz |
| n(t) | Standard Gaussian random process | randn() in MATLAB |

The **theoretical noise power** is:

$$P_{noise} = 4kTRB$$

Thermal noise has a **flat Power Spectral Density** (white noise) — equal power at all frequencies within the bandwidth.

| PSD Shape | Flat (white noise) | Flat ✅ |

---

## 🔬 Observations

**Time Domain:**
- Noise amplitude fluctuates randomly around zero
- No periodic pattern — purely random
- RMS value matches the theoretical √(4kTRB)

**PSD Plot:**
- Flat horizontal line across all frequencies = white noise
- Confirms thermal noise has equal power at all frequencies within bandwidth

**Histogram:**
- Bell-shaped (Gaussian/Normal) distribution
- Centered at zero with spread equal to the RMS noise voltage

## 🧪 Experimentation — Effect of Parameters

To explore effects on noise, try modifying these values in the code:

| Parameter | Increase Effect | Decrease Effect |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| Temperature T ↑ | Higher noise power, wider histogram | Quieter noise |
| Resistance R ↑ | Higher noise voltage | Lower noise voltage |
| Bandwidth B ↑ | More noise samples, higher power | Narrower spectrum |

A commented-out section in the code (`Figure 4`) lets you compare PSDs at different temperatures — just uncomment it to see the effect.

## 🔑 Key MATLAB Commands Used

| Command | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| `randn(1, n)` | Generates n Gaussian random samples (mean=0, std=1) |
| `pwelch(x,[],[],[],Fs)` | Estimates Power Spectral Density using Welch's method |
| `semilogx(f, P)` | Plots PSD with logarithmic frequency axis |
| `histogram(x, bins)` | Plots amplitude distribution |
| `mean()`, `std()` | Compute statistical properties of noise |
